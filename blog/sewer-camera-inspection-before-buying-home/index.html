    Why a Sewer Camera Inspection Belongs on Every Kentucky Home Inspection

    By Andrew Blake · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

    Protecting Your Investment: Beyond the Visible

    When you're buying a home in Kentucky, you understandably focus on what you can see: the roof, the foundation, the appliances, the paint. A traditional home inspection covers these important areas. However, there's a whole network of pipes hidden underground that are just as vital to a home's functionality and your peace of mind. Without a close look, these buried sewer lines can harbor expensive problems that only surface after you've closed on the house. That's why at BFK Plumbing, we strongly recommend a sewer camera inspection as a must-have part of every home inspection in Kentucky.

    Our inspection process uses special waterproof cameras, often equipped with powerful LED lights, inserted directly into the main sewer line through a cleanout access point. This allows our trained technicians to navigate the pipe's interior and record its condition in real time. We can spot everything from minor blockages to major structural damage, giving you a clear view of what lies beneath before you commit to purchasing.

    Common Sewer Line Issues in Kentucky Homes

    Much of Kentucky has karst terrain with limestone bedrock and sinkholes, which can complicate sewer line and drainage work.

    Older properties and homes built on expansive clay soils are especially susceptible to certain sewer line issues. Here's what we regularly find:

    • Root Intrusion: Mature trees are common in established neighborhoods, and their thirsty roots naturally seek out water sources, including the moisture inside sewer pipes. Even tiny cracks can allow roots to enter, grow, and eventually create serious blockages or even burst pipes.
    • Shifting Soil and Foundation Issues: Clay soils expand when wet and contract when dry. This constant movement can put heavy pressure on underground pipes, leading to cracks, dislodged sections, or misaligned joints. Foundation issues often go hand-in-hand with drain problems.
    • Corrosion and Deterioration: Many older homes still have cast iron or clay tile sewer pipes. Over decades, these materials can corrode, crack, or collapse. A camera inspection can reveal the extent of this damage, which is often invisible from the surface.
    • Bellied Pipes: This occurs when a section of the pipe sags, creating a low spot where water and waste can collect, leading to repeated clogs. This is often a result of poor installation or significant ground movement over time.
    • Grease and Sludge Buildup: Even in newer homes, years of improper disposal of grease and other materials can lead to heavy buildup, narrowing the pipe's diameter and increasing the risk of backups.

    Newer construction mostly uses PEX and plastic drains, while older houses often have a mix of materials from past repairs.

    The Cost of Ignoring Your Sewer Lines

    Imagine moving into your new Kentucky home only to have the drains back up a few weeks later. The cost of emergency repairs, including excavation to access and replace damaged sections of pipe, can easily range from a few hundred dollars for a minor repair to several thousand or even tens of thousands for a full line replacement. A sewer camera inspection, by contrast, typically costs between $200 and $400. This relatively small investment can save you from a major financial headache and significant stress down the road. Knowing the condition of the sewer line upfront gives you powerful leverage during negotiations, allowing you to request repairs or a credit from the seller.

    When to Call a Professional

    Anytime you are purchasing a home in Kentucky, especially one built before 1990, a sewer camera inspection is a wise decision. If the home shows signs of previous drain issues, has large trees in the yard, or if you simply want complete peace of mind, don't skip this key step.     The Peace of Mind a Camera Inspection Provides

    A complete home inspection should cover all major components, and in today's market, that absolutely includes the main sewer line. Think of it as an MRI for your home's underground arteries. Without this detailed view, you're buying a property with a significant blind spot. With a sewer camera inspection from BFK Plumbing, you gain valuable information about the integrity of the plumbing system. This information empowers you to make an informed purchasing decision, negotiate effectively, and avoid costly surprises after you've unpacked your boxes.
