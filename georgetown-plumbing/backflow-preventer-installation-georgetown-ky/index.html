    Reliable Backflow Preventer Installation Services in Georgetown, KY

    Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Installation in Georgetown, KY

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of protection and are required for high hazard connections like commercial kitchens, medical facilities, chemical injection and car washes. They include two independent check valves with a relief valve that dumps water if either check fails. Because the relief valve can discharge, we plan for drainage and install the assembly above grade where it can be tested. We carry trusted brands like Watts, Febco, Wilkins and Apollo.

    Backflow Testing and Certification in Georgetown

    Most water providers require testable backflow assemblies to be certified at installation and then annually. Our certified testers check each valve's opening point, relief valve operation and differential pressure. If a device fails, we can repair or rebuild it right away with genuine parts. We submit passing results so you avoid compliance letters or service interruption.

    Commercial Backflow Prevention in Georgetown, KY

    Commercial properties in Georgetown, KY often have complex plumbing systems that pose a higher risk of cross-contamination and need robust backflow prevention. Restaurants, hospitals, industrial facilities and even multi-family buildings must comply with commercial plumbing codes. BFK Plumbing provides complete installation of heavy-duty backflow preventers, including large diameter RPZ assemblies, to safeguard public health and meet Kentucky regulations for businesses. We handle all necessary permits and inspections.

    Double Check Valve Assembly Installation in Georgetown

    Double check assemblies are used for low hazard connections such as many fire sprinkler systems and some irrigation lines. They have two spring-loaded check valves and test cocks for annual certification. Unlike an RPZ, they do not discharge water, so they can be installed in vaults or below grade where local rules permit. We size them to avoid too much pressure loss on your system.

    Commercial Backflow Prevention Systems in Georgetown, KY

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Device Installation in Georgetown, KY

    For high-hazard applications in Georgetown, KY where toxic substances could contaminate the water supply, a reduced pressure zone, or RPZ, device is often required. RPZ backflow preventers offer the highest level of protection, using a relief valve to discharge water if the pressure drops. BFK Plumbing is experienced with RPZ installation, making sure all components are correctly sized, positioned and set up according to state and local plumbing codes. Proper installation is critical for effective operation.

    Double Check Valve Assembly (DCVA) Installation in Georgetown

    The double check valve assembly, or DCVA, is a widely used backflow preventer for low-hazard applications where the consequences of backflow are not severe. In Georgetown, DCVAs are often installed on fire sprinkler systems or irrigation lines. Our certified plumbers precisely install these devices, making sure both check valves operate correctly and are properly sealed to prevent backpressure or back-siphonage into the main water supply.

    Backflow Enclosures and Freeze Protection in Georgetown, KY

    Above-ground assemblies sit in direct sun in summer and can freeze on cold nights. Most Kentucky homeowners disconnect hoses and protect outdoor spigots in the fall, and many insulate vulnerable pipes in crawlspaces. We install insulated enclosures, freeze blankets or heated cabinets based on your location. Enclosures also discourage theft, since brass backflow assemblies are a common target for scrap thieves. We choose enclosures with enough room for testing and service.

    Signs of a failing backflow preventer include discolored or foul-smelling water, a noticeable drop in water pressure, visible leaks around the device or water discharging from the relief valve on an RPZ. If you notice any of these, contact us for an inspection in Georgetown.

    Most water providers require testable assemblies to be tested at installation and then annually. Some high hazard facilities may be tested more often.
