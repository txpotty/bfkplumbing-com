    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Florence

    The cost of underground pipe repair in Florence, KY can vary widely, typically ranging from $500 for minor spot repairs to $3,000 to $10,000 or more for trenchless full line replacements, or even more for extensive excavation and re-piping. Factors include the pipe material, depth, length of the damaged section, the repair method chosen and the extent of property restoration needed. We provide honest, upfront pricing after a thorough diagnosis.

    For water lines we typically use PEX or Type K copper, both approved for direct burial. For sewer lines we use Schedule 40 PVC. The right choice depends on the existing pipe and local code.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's often faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, often extending the lifespan of your pipes.

    Ignoring an underground pipe leak can lead to significant problems, including costly water damage to your foundation, landscaping erosion, mold growth, higher water bills and potential contamination of your water supply. It's important to address these issues promptly.

    BFK Plumbing uses high-quality, industry-approved materials from trusted brands known for durability and longevity. This includes PEX piping from manufacturers like Uponor or Viega, and various PVC and ABS pipe products that meet ASTM standards.

    Common signs of a broken underground pipe include unexplained wet spots or lush patches in your yard, a sudden drop in water pressure, unusual odors such as sulfur or sewage, gurgling sounds from drains or toilets or an unexpected increase in your water bill. If you notice any of these, it's smart to call for a professional inspection promptly.

    We use acoustic listening devices that pick up the sound of escaping water, tracer gas that rises through soil at the leak point and electronic locators that trace the pipe path. Together these narrow the leak to a small area.
