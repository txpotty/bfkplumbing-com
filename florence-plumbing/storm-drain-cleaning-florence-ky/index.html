    Frequently Asked Questions About Storm Drain Cleaning in Florence

    The cost of storm drain cleaning in Florence typically ranges from $200 to $600, depending on the severity and type of blockage, the length of the drain, and the equipment required, such as hydro jetting or snaking. More complex issues like extensive root intrusion or collapsed pipes may cost more. We provide a written, upfront estimate before starting any work.

    Hydro jetting is a powerful drain cleaning method that uses high-pressure water streams to blast away stubborn blockages and thoroughly clean the interior of drain pipes. For storm drains, it is highly effective at removing compacted soil, heavy silt, grease, and roots, restoring the drain to its full flow capacity much better than snaking.

    Cleaning does not need a permit. New dry wells or major drainage changes may require permits or registration, and we can guide you.

    Our technicians are trained to service all types and brands of storm drain systems typically found in residential and commercial properties. This includes standard PVC, corrugated HDPE, concrete, and cast iron drainpipes, as well as various types of catch basins and grates from manufacturers like NDS, ADS, and Zurn.

    Common signs of a clogged storm drain include standing water in your yard or driveway after rain, water overflowing from catch basins, unusual pooling near downspouts, or slow drainage in outdoor areas. You might also notice debris piling up around drain grates, a sign the system is failing to clear water.

    We recommend having your storm drains inspected and possibly cleaned once a year, especially if you have mature trees nearby. River flooding and saturated ground can cause sewer backups, so backwater valves and sump pumps are worthwhile upgrades. Properties prone to debris buildup or with a history of clogs may need twice-yearly cleaning to prevent major issues.

    You can clear grates and remove surface debris with a scoop. Pipes packed with silt and gravel typically need jetting and vacuum equipment.

    Most homes take 1 to 3 hours. Large commercial sites or dry wells may take a half day or longer.
