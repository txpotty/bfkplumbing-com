    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Florence

    Small indoor grease traps usually cost $150 to $400 per cleaning. Large outdoor interceptors often run $300 to $1,200 depending on size and buildup. Regular maintenance plans can lower the cost per visit.

    Generally, you do not need a permit to have your existing grease trap cleaned by a professional. However, commercial establishments in Florence are often required to maintain records of their grease trap cleaning for compliance audits. New grease trap installations might require plumbing permits.

    Neglecting grease trap cleaning leads to severe FOG buildup, causing slow drains, complete plumbing blockages, foul odors, and potential sewage backups into your kitchen. It can also result in costly fines from health and environmental agencies in Florence, KY.

    Staff can sometimes clean very small under-sink traps, but waste must be disposed of properly. Outdoor interceptors require a vacuum truck and a licensed hauler, and many cities require professional service records.

    Local regulations in Florence, KY often require commercial food service establishments to install and regularly maintain grease traps or interceptors. These regulations specify minimum cleaning frequencies, proper waste disposal, and may require permits or inspections. We make sure our services help you meet these mandates.

    The cleaning frequency depends heavily on your business's FOG output and the size of your trap. Most restaurants require cleaning every 1 to 3 months, while high-volume establishments might need it monthly. Local health codes in Florence may also specify minimum frequencies.

    BFK Plumbing services all common types of grease traps and interceptors, including passive hydromechanical grease interceptors (HGIs), automatic grease removal units (AGRUs), and large gravity grease interceptors (GGIs). We handle both indoor under-sink units and outdoor in-ground tanks.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, adhering to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.
