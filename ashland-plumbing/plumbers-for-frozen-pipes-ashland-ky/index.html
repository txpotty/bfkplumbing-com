    Common signs of a frozen pipe include a sudden loss of water pressure, no water coming out of a faucet, or unusual noises like gurgling or banging from your pipes. You might also see visible frost on exposed pipes or notice a damp spot on a wall or ceiling if a slow leak has started.

    On the coldest nights, letting faucets drip and opening cabinet doors on outside walls can prevent a burst pipe. Pipes can freeze anywhere temperatures fall below freezing for several hours, especially in attics, crawl spaces and exterior walls with little insulation.     While any pipe can freeze, older galvanized steel or copper pipes in uninsulated areas are usually more susceptible to bursting due to their rigid material.     Ice can split a pipe while frozen, but the leak only appears once the ice melts and water flows again. That is why we pressure test every thawed line. It catches hidden splits before they do damage.

    To prevent pipes from freezing in Ashland, insulate all exposed pipes, especially those in unheated areas like crawl spaces, attics, and basements.
