    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Louisville

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves freely. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats suit larger pits.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Louisville

    If your current sump pump struggles to keep up with heavy water flow, especially during intense thunderstorms, it may be undersized. We offer high-capacity sump pump replacements and upgrades designed to handle significant water volumes. Our plumbers will assess your water intrusion patterns and recommend a pump with greater horsepower and a higher GPM (gallons per minute) rating. This prevents the pump from cycling too often or failing under stress, common issues in some Louisville properties.

    Commercial Pit Pump Replacement in Louisville

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Louisville

    The cost of sump pump replacement in Louisville, KY typically ranges from $500 to $1,500, depending on the type of pump, submersible or pedestal, its horsepower and any necessary pit changes. Battery backup systems will add to this cost, usually between $300 and $800. We provide a written, upfront quote after assessing your needs.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by BFK Plumbing can also help lengthen its lifespan.

    Manufacturer warranties typically range from 3 to 5 years. We also offer a labor warranty.

    Key signs include the pump not turning on, unusual noises such as grinding or rattling, running nonstop, visible rust or corrosion, or an age over 7 to 10 years. Any of these could point to coming failure and a need for replacement.

    It is recommended to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional inspection.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to activate automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is critical for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a wise investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Louisville homes are better off with a backup system due to storm risks.
