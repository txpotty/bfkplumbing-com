    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Frankfort

    Most faucet repairs cost $125 to $300, including the cartridge or seals and labor. Complex kitchen or touchless faucet repairs may run $200 to $400. We quote a firm price before any work starts.

    For a standard faucet repair, where only internal components are replaced and the fixture itself remains, no plumbing permits are typically required in Frankfort. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or installing a new type of fixture that requires new connections, a permit might be necessary. We will advise you if any permits are needed for your specific situation.

    Much of Kentucky sits on limestone, so water tends to run moderately hard to hard, leaving scale in water heaters and fixtures over time. Hard water contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up inside your faucets, corroding internal parts, clogging aerators, and stiffening moving components. This often leads to leaks, reduced water pressure, and a shorter faucet lifespan, making regular maintenance or timely repair important.

    Most standard faucet repairs, such as replacing a washer or cartridge, can be completed by an skilled plumber in about 30 minutes to an hour. More complex issues or specialty faucets might take longer, but we strive for efficient service to minimize disruption to your home in Frankfort.

    A worn cartridge, washer or valve seat is the usual culprit. Hard water scale makes these parts wear out faster, so drips are common in many homes.

    The most common signs include a persistent drip or leak, difficulty turning the handles, inconsistent water temperature, reduced water pressure from the faucet, or strange noises when operating it. Any of these could indicate internal component wear or damage requiring attention.

    Low water pressure can stem from several issues, including mineral deposits clogging the aerator or internal components, a worn-out cartridge, issues with your home's main water supply, or even problems with municipal water pressure. Our technicians perform a careful diagnosis to accurately pinpoint and resolve the cause.

    If the faucet body is solid and parts are available, a repair is generally worth it. If it is corroded, cracked or discontinued, a new faucet is often the better choice.
