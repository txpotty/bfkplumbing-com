    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Jeffersontown

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the available water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the best ways to extend pump life.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Jeffersontown, KY

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Jeffersontown

    Most well pressure tank replacements cost $900 to $2,200 installed, depending on tank size and whether the switch and tank tee are replaced. Large capacity or multi-tank setups can run $2,500 or more. We give a written price before starting.

    A well pressure tank usually lasts between 7 to 15 years, depending on water quality, usage, and maintenance. Tanks in areas with harder water or higher demand may have a shorter lifespan. Regular inspection can help catch potential issues before complete failure.

    A like-for-like tank replacement often does not require a permit in most counties. Electrical changes or new piping may, and we check local rules for you.

    Rapid pump cycling, pressure that swings up and down, and water coming out of the air valve are common signs. A tank that sounds solid and full when tapped near the top is usually waterlogged.

    We install Amtrol Well-X-Trol, Flexcon and Water Worker tanks. All three are easy to source and backed by solid manufacturer warranties.

    On average, a well pressure tank can last between 5 to 10 years, though some high-quality tanks can last longer. Factors like water quality, proper initial installation, and regular maintenance can greatly impact its lifespan.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, fully isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are often preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.
