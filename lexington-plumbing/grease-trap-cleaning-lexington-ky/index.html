    The cost of grease trap cleaning in Lexington can range from $200 to $800 or more, depending on the size of the trap, its accessibility, and the level of FOG accumulation.     Neglecting grease trap cleaning leads to severe FOG buildup, causing slow drains, complete plumbing blockages, foul odors, and potential sewage backups into your kitchen. It can also result in costly fines from health and environmental agencies in Lexington, KY.

    Key signs include slow drains in your kitchen sink, gurgling noises from drains, foul odors coming from your drains or around the trap area, and visible FOG buildup when you inspect the trap. Ignoring these can lead to complete blockages.

    Neglecting commercial grease trap cleaning in Lexington can lead to severe drain blockages, sewage backups, foul odors impacting your business, and potential fines from the local health department or wastewater authority. It can also cause expensive plumbing emergencies and business downtime.

    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' usually refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models. Both serve the same function of separating FOG from wastewater.

    The duration of a grease trap cleaning can vary, but most standard commercial cleanings take between 1 to 3 hours. Larger outdoor interceptors or traps with significant buildup may take longer. We aim to complete the service quickly to minimize disruption to your business operations in Lexington.

    The cleaning frequency depends heavily on your business's FOG output and the size of your trap. Most restaurants require cleaning every 1 to 3 months, while high-volume establishments might need it monthly. Local health codes in Lexington might also specify minimum frequencies.

    FOG stands for Fats, Oils, and Grease. When poured down drains, FOG cools and solidifies, sticking to pipe walls and eventually leading to severe clogs. In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.
