    Expansion Tanks With Tankless Water Heaters in Lexington, KY

    Tankless water heaters hold very little water, so thermal expansion is minimal in most homes. However, homes with recirculation loops, storage buffer tanks or certain closed system setups may still benefit from an expansion tank. We evaluate your system and only recommend a tank when it serves a genuine purpose. This keeps you from paying for parts you do not need.

    Expansion Tank Sizing for Large Water Heaters in Lexington

    A standard 2 gallon tank works for many 40 and 50 gallon heaters at moderate pressure. Larger heaters, higher incoming pressure or high-capacity systems may need a 4.5 gallon tank or larger. We use manufacturer sizing charts that factor in heater volume, supply pressure and temperature setting. Correct sizing keeps the tank from over-flexing and failing early.

    Expansion Tank Installation Cost Comparison in Lexington, KY

    Adding an expansion tank during a water heater replacement is the most cost-effective approach because the water is already off and the piping is open. Installing one as a standalone job costs a bit more in labor but still pays for itself by protecting the heater. Larger tanks, attic installs and PRV replacement add to the total. We provide a clear price before any work starts.

    Replacing Old Expansion Tanks in Lexington

    Expansion tanks do not last forever, and an old, waterlogged or corroded tank offers no protection. If your existing tank in Lexington is showing signs of failure, such as a dripping relief valve or fluctuating water pressure, it is time for a replacement. We expertly remove the old unit, dispose of it responsibly and install a new, correctly sized expansion tank. This timely replacement prevents costly damage to your water heater and plumbing system and restores proper pressure control.

    Pressure Reducing Valve and Expansion Tank Pairing in Lexington

    Many homes see high incoming pressure, so pressure reducing valves are common. The PRV protects your fixtures but also turns your plumbing into a closed system, which makes an expansion tank necessary. We test the PRV output and replace it if it is creeping or failing. Pairing a healthy PRV with a correctly charged expansion tank gives you steady pressure throughout the house.
