    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Paducah

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the usable water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the most effective ways to extend pump life.

    Large Capacity Tanks for Ranch and Commercial Wells in Paducah

    Properties with livestock, irrigation or more than one home on a well need more storage than a single residential tank. We install multiple tanks manifolded together or large capacity models over 100 gallons. Proper sizing keeps big three-phase pumps from cycling too often.     A well pressure tank typically lasts between 7 to 15 years, depending on water quality, usage, and maintenance. Tanks in areas with harder water or higher demand may have a shorter lifespan. Regular inspection can help catch potential issues before complete failure.

    Yes, absolutely. A failing well pressure tank often causes the well pump to short-cycle, meaning it turns on and off too frequently. This constant starting and stopping puts heavy strain on the pump's motor, leading to premature wear and potential failure of the well pump itself.

    Common signs include your well pump constantly turning on and off (short cycling), fluctuating water pressure, air sputtering from faucets, or a tank that is waterlogged (feels heavy when tapped and full of water). If you notice any of these, it is time for an inspection.

    We install a variety of trusted well pressure tank brands, including Well-Rite, Flex-Lite, Amtrol, and Goulds. Our technicians can recommend the best brand and model for your specific well system and budget.

    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, completely isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    Regular maintenance for a well pressure tank involves checking the air pressure in the tank at least once a year, ensuring it's properly pre-charged. You should also listen for excessive pump cycling and visually inspect the tank for any signs of corrosion or leaks. We can include this check during regular well system servicing.
